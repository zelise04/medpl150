Reflection

Our bodies are contaminated by tiny pieces of plastic that cannot break down naturally. Scientists and medical experts have found links between these plastics and susceptibility to health problems. Despite the widely known adverse effects of plastic, it remains one of the world's most heavily produced materials. Plastic is the most popular material for food packaging. Its close contact with the food we eat provides an ideal pathway for plastic particles to enter our bodies. Microplastics have been found in every organ system in the human body and research has shown that they affect human health significantly. Microplastics may make certain conditions worse or increase the risk of disease in individuals already predisposed to them. As someone who lives with a chronic illness, learning this information is especially upsetting. Even though microplastics are not directly linked to causing diseases, they can impact the body’s capability to protect itself against illnesses. I often wonder if I wasn’t exposed to plastics and other harmful materials my whole life, would my condition have a lesser severity? Would I even have this condition at all?

My photomontage uses a combination of stock images found on Wikimedia Commons and a photo of my hand holding various medications. I first wanted to address the irony of washing down prescription medications with water from a plastic bottle. I also wanted to touch on the environmental impacts of single use plastics and I did this by putting a picture of a landfill in Ghana within the water bottle. I used masking here to isolate the water bottle, my hand, and the image of the landfill in the water bottle. In illustrator, I used the transparency blend mode to make the words in the background more opaque. I didn’t want them to contrast too much with the subject of my image, so softening the tone with the opacity tool was useful.
