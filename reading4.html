Reading 4 - Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It

“Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It” was an extremely eye-opening piece on the reality of nuclear war and the devastating impacts that even one detonated nuclear warhead would have on the earth. Before getting into my response to this article, I would first like to discuss the design choices made by the team at NYT. I felt it was really impactful to incorporate interactive and visual elements in this article. I was especially amazed by the illustrations that accompanied that section talking about the immediate effects of the detonation of a nuclear weapon. The vivid colors of the explosion contrasted with the murky hues of the devastated city really felt like I was brought into a post-apocalyptic reality. I also was fascinated by the section that explained the history of nuclear weapon production through a map of the world. It was interesting seeing the dramatic increase in the number of nuclear warheads during the cold war. The eventual dwindling in the 2000s was a bit refreshing, but it was accompanied by more countries developing nuclear programs and text that explained that even though the numbers went down, the technology was becoming more advanced. I felt like this was a great way to get an important point across, especially in an age when most people can't pay attention to long blocks of text anymore.

A nuclear fallout has been a fear of mine since I was little. I remember seeing signs for a “Fallout Shelter” in the stairway leading to the basement of my elementary school and slowly becoming more fearful of these signs as I became more educated on our nation’s history. When the war in Ukraine first started, I remember hearing a lot about the potential for a nuclear weapon being used and I felt a lot of anxiety about the increasing potential for a “world ending event.” The developments in the beginning of that war, in my opinion, were scarier than COVID, which I felt I at least had some control over (e.g. wearing a mask, getting vaccinated, etc.) I can’t imagine how Ukrainians felt at the time, and maybe even now since the war is still ongoing. I feel like, overall, that top officials who control wars and aggression have lost a significant portion of their morality, or even humanity. To threaten the world with a devastating event that would not only initially destroy thousands, if not millions of lives, but also have lasting effects for decades just to make a point is just extremely evil. The whole world saw the effects of Hiroshima and instances of other types of chemical weapons (like agent orange), and to still think that these weapons are a “trump card” to simply throw around is insane.
