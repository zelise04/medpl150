Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis Reflection

In the graphic novel “Motherhood in Crisis” four women from Sierra Leone share their stories about pregnancy and motherhood in a country that has limited resources to support healthy and complex conditions. We are first introduced to Memunatu, a mother who experiences unexplained heavy bleeding after a stillbirth. She grapples with the loss of her child and her new reality of constant pain and leaking. She was visited by an advocate who provided comfort to her as they shared the same medical condition. Memunatu was brought to the hospital and they discovered that she had a fistula. She had to wait a long time for a western doctor to arrive and perform an operation. Eventually, Memanatu had undergone surgery and was sent home. She had another child and had to give birth at the hospital due to her condition. I thought this was a very poignant story and I was especially moved by Memanatu being able to have another child despite the threat of complications. The next story is about Heela, a school girl who is raped by an older man. She became pregnant and didn’t know if she wanted to keep the baby, especially because that would mean she wouldn't be able to continue schooling. This story was very upsetting. Heels’s age is unknown but I assumed that there was a large age gap between the man who assaulted her and herself. It was upsetting that she didn’t receive justice because the man ran away from the village upon hearing about her pregnancy. This was great insight on what happens in developing countries when a pregnancy is unwanted.

The third woman we are introduced to is Kadiatu. She experienced great loss in her life. She gave birth to 6 children and lost 5 of them. She received a lot of shame from her community because they thought she was sacrificing her children for occult practices. The community itself was very weary of medical care, as they were still traumatized by what happened during the Ebola epidemic. They believed that any time a person was transported out of the village or visited by a medical professional, they would die. Kadiatu had to overcome these fears due to an unexpected complication in her 7th pregnancy. She gave birth at the hospital via c-section and was relieved to know that she would be able to return home. I thought this was an interesting story on how traditional practices often clash with modern medicine. While there is nothing wrong with traditional medicine or healers, I feel like there should be a balance between tradition and science. Finally, the last story was about Tetteh, a woman who was pregnant while being imprisoned. Her story was very upsetting, as I feel like she shouldn’t have been in jail in the first place. She killed her abuser, who constantly put her life and her baby’s life at risk. I hope that Tetteh was able to receive justice and be reconnected with her son.
