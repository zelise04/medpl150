"With ‘AI slop’ distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster" by Nesrine Malik

I think for a lot of people it has been increasingly difficult to differentiate AI from “real life.” Everyday I hear the question, “Is this real or AI?”, from a multitude of sources, spanning from my family to overheard conversations on my daily commute. In the past, believing or seeing something that is not real would be considered a hallucination of some sort. Now, all of us struggle to figure out if what we are seeing online and even in “real life” is real or fake. As Nesrine mentioned in her article, it seems that we have become split between two worlds: one in which there are photographs and reports of the world as it is, and another where a machine learning model tries to imitate processes that occur offline.

It's concerning that so many people use, and even trust, AI for their news, facts, and information. Any place where you can access an AI chatbot has a small disclaimer that their LLM “might make mistakes” or “may not always be accurate.” I have learned that the best AI models out there have been achieving a little less than 50% accuracy rate in official tests. The chat bots readily available for public use are not the cream of the crop, so I can’t even imagine what their accuracy rates are. Some AI chat bots are still unable to search the internet or access real-time information, which makes it even more infuriating that so many people use these technologies to be “up-to-date” with everything going on in the world.

As Nesrine mentioned, however, misinformation created by AI is, “simply an extension of traditional propaganda.” When I was younger, I remember there being a big online “war” between people who watched Fox News vs. people who watch CNN. Both sides were calling each other’s preferred channel “fake news” and claiming that these broadcasters would manipulate stories to fit an agenda that they were pushing. Now, I feel like there is a lot of tension between people who use AI as their news source and people who get their news from traditional outlets. No matter where you get your news from, I feel like it will always be manipulated in some way. So, even though AI is for the most part inaccurate, how do you know if “real” news is inaccurate in some way?

To answer my own question, I feel that “real” news has a lot of oversight compared to AI. AI will shoot out an answer in less than 30 seconds and is not programmed to question if its responses are accurate or not. Before a news article or story is finally published, it usually undergoes review by multiple people. Although some outlets may intentionally obscure or omit certain details to promote a specific agenda, the overarching goal for most is to present information as clearly as they can.
